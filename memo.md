# Memo: Where the Business Is Losing Profit

**To:** Head of Retail Operations
**From:** Hansika Garg
**Data:** Superstore orders, 2014–2017 (9,994 order lines, 5,009 orders)

## Recommendation
Cap discounts at 20% and review pricing on Tables and Bookcases. Discounts above 20% are the single biggest source of lost profit in this dataset.

## What the data shows
- **Overall:** sales of about $2.30M and profit of about $286K, a margin of 12.5%.
- **Discounts above 20% lose money.** 1,393 order lines (13.9% of all lines, 15.8% of sales) made a combined loss of about **$135K**. Lines with no discount or a discount up to 20% were profitable (about +$321K and +$101K).
- **Losses get worse as discounts rise.** Profit by discount band: 21–40% = about -$36K; over 40% = about -$100K.
- **Tables is a discounting problem, not a demand problem.** Tables lost about $17.7K overall. With no discount it made about +$13K, at 20% or less it roughly broke even, and above 20% it lost about $31K.
- **Central region has the weakest margin (7.9%).** It has the highest average discount (24%) and 28% of its lines are discounted above 20%. West, with the best margin (14.9%), discounts above 20% on only 4% of lines.
- **Furniture earns a 2.0% margin**, against 17% for Office Supplies and Technology.

## Estimated impact
If discounts above 20% had been capped and those 1,393 lines had only broken even, profit would have been about **$135K higher (+47%)**. If they had earned the same 11.9% margin as lines discounted up to 20%, the gain would be about **$179K (+62%)**.

## Assumptions and risks
- The estimate assumes the same sales volume at lower discounts. Some customers may buy less or not at all, so treat these figures as upper-end estimates.
- The data covers 2014–2017 and does not include costs beyond those built into the profit column.
- A discount cap should be tested first, for example in the Central region, before rolling out everywhere.

## Next steps
1. Pilot a 20% discount cap in the Central region for one quarter and track sales volume and profit.
2. Review pricing and discount approvals for Tables and Bookcases.
3. Report profit margin by discount band monthly.
